Procedural terrain generation is the intersection of noise functions and rendering — a heightmap derived from mathematical noise, shaded to look like a real landscape. No artist-painted textures, no hand-sculpted geometry. Everything is a function of position.

## Implementation

**Fractal Brownian Motion (fBm)** layers multiple octaves of Perlin noise at increasing frequency and decreasing amplitude. The result is terrain with both large-scale mountain ranges and fine-grained surface detail.

**Surface normals** are computed using central-difference height gradients — sample the heightmap at four neighboring points and cross the differences. This avoids storing explicit normals and updates automatically when parameters change.

**Biome shading** maps elevation to five geological bands — water, sand, grass, rock, and snow — with smooth blending at transitions. The shader reads elevation directly from the vertex and interpolates between palette colors.

**Atmospheric fog** fades distant geometry toward a sky color based on fragment depth, adding depth cues without post-processing.

## Lessons Learned

- **Noise is composable** — fBm is just weighted sums of noise at different scales, but the visual result is dramatically richer than any single octave.
- **Normals from finite differences** — numerical normal calculation is simpler and more flexible than storing face normals, especially for dynamic geometry.
- **Wireframe mode is a debugging tool** — seeing the mesh triangle density reveals where resolution is wasted.
