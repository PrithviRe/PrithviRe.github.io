Particle systems are the simplest form of large-scale simulation — each particle has a position, velocity, and lifetime, and the visual result comes from rendering hundreds of thousands of them with the right blending mode.

## Implementation

**Preallocated buffers** — the particle pool is fixed-size, allocated once at startup. No `new`, no `std::vector::push_back` in the hot loop. Dead particles are recycled by overwriting their state.

**Dynamic vertex streaming** — `glBufferSubData` uploads the live particle positions each frame into a persistent VBO. The GPU renders them as hardware point sprites using `gl_PointCoord` for per-fragment circular falloff.

**Additive blending** — `GL_SRC_ALPHA, GL_ONE` causes overlapping particles to brighten rather than occlude, producing the characteristic glow effect where particle density is highest.

**Interactive forces** — radial inverse-distance cursor attraction pulls particles inward; a click triggers a repulsive impulse that sends them scattering. Lifetime decay gradually fades and respawns particles.

## Lessons Learned

- **Point sprites scale** — hardware point sprites avoid the geometry overhead of billboarded quads entirely.
- **Additive blending is free visual complexity** — overlapping particles create emergent light effects with no extra computation.
- **Preallocated pools eliminate GC pauses** — fixed-size arrays with index recycling keep the frame time flat.
